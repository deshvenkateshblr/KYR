Significance of Pitru Paksha

According to Madhwa (Dvaita) principles, Pitru Devatas are primarily the divine Pit?s—celestial beings regarded as the grandchildren of Chaturmukha Brahma—who occupy a defined place in the Taratamya hierarchy. These include the Sapta Pitru Ganas (seven groups such as the Vairajas, Agni?vattas and Barhi?ads) and the Pitru Patis (Yama, Soma and Kavyavaha). They receive the offerings made during ancestral rites and convey them to the departed human ancestors (the Dvadasha Pit?s of three generations on the paternal and maternal sides), who are invoked in the forms of Vasu, Rudra and Aditya, with the Antaryami forms of Pradyumna, Sa?kar?a?a and Vasudeva as the ultimate indwellers.Pitru Paksha, also known as Mahalaya Paksha, is the sacred dark fortnight (Krishna Paksha) of the lunar month of Bhadrapada, usually falling in September–October. In the Madhwa tradition this period is especially important for honouring both the divine Pitru Devatas and one’s human ancestors (including the Karu?ya Pit?s). Performing Tarpa?a and at least one Paksha Sraddha—preferably on the ancestor’s tithi, Mahabhara?i, Madhya??ami or Mahalaya Amavasya—satisfies the Pit?s, helps discharge Pitru ??a, and invites their blessings of prosperity, health and continuity of the family lineage.

The ritual transcends immediate family lines—offering recognition not only to direct parents and grandparents, but extending across twelve primary ancestral positions (Dwadasha Pitrugalu), maternal kin, teachers, mentors, and extended relations who contributed to our spiritual and physical existence.

Core Rituals & Observances

Tarpanam

The sacred offering of water mixed with black sesame seeds (Tila) and darbha grass, invoking the peace and satisfaction of ancestral spirits across gotras.

Pinda Pradana

The consecrated offering of rice balls mixed with sesame seeds, barley, and ghee. Each pinda represents a defined seat of lineage and gratitude, laid out in an authentic, strict canonical order.

The Structure of Offerings

The app follows traditional liturgical hierarchy:

  • Pitru Varga: Three generations of paternal male ancestors paired with their spouses (Father, Grandfather, Great-grandfather, and their wives), plus Step-mother if applicable.
  • Maataamaha Varga: Three generations of maternal male ancestors paired with their spouses (Mother's Father, Grandfather, Great-grandfather, and their wives).
  • Other Pitrugalu: Extended lineage spanning siblings, aunts, uncles, in-laws, disciples, preceptors (Gurus), and friends.
